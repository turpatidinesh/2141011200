Linux was created in 1991 by Linus Torvalds, a student from Finland, who wanted a free and open-source alternative to the UNIX operating system. He released the Linux kernel and invited programmers worldwide to contribute. At the same time, the GNU Project, started in 1983 by Richard Stallman, was already developing free software tools and utilities. When combined with the Linux kernel, the full operating system became known as GNU/Linux.
The philosophy of GNU/Linux centers around the idea of software freedom — the belief that users should have the right to run, modify, share, and study software. This open-source model promotes transparency, collaboration, and community development. It empowers users and developers to create secure, customizable, and efficient systems without being tied to proprietary restrictions.
